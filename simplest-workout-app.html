On first launch Alea shows a single welcome sheet explaining the dice, the streak and duels in three lines. Its button says "Roll the dice". After that it never appears again, and the app opens straight onto the roll.

Why twenty exercises beats a thousand

Every competing app advertises the size of its exercise library. Alea has twenty movements, and that is a deliberate ceiling rather than a shortfall.

A thousand-exercise library is a thousand decisions. You do not browse it; you scroll it, feel vaguely guilty, and pick the same six things you always pick. A small, ordered pool means the dice can decide for you, and a die is only meaningful when the choices behind it are bounded.

The pool is ordered, so the die is a difficulty dial

Exercises run from foundational to advanced, and the die slices from the top. A D4 draws only push-ups, pull-ups, squats and burpees. A D20 opens the whole list including pistol squats and muscle-ups. One control, no menus.

No feed, no social graph, no badges

There is no activity feed, no follower count and no achievement wall. The only social feature is a duel, and it works by sending someone a link. Nothing to maintain and nothing to catch up on.

What simple costs you

Simplicity is a trade, not a free win, and this is where Alea genuinely loses to the subscription apps.

  • No exercise demonstrations. If you draw archer push-ups and do not know the movement, the app names it and nothing more. Every paid competitor has video.
  • No progression system. Nothing periodises your training or tells you when to advance. The dice do not know how strong you are.
  • No coaching. No form feedback, no plan, no adaptation to your goals.
  • No integrations. Nothing syncs to Health Connect, Google Fit or a watch.

If you want a structured programme that takes you from your first pull-up to a muscle-up with video at every step, buy one of the subscription apps. They are good, and that is a real job Alea does not do.

Alea is for the other problem: the days you were not going to train at all, because deciding what to do was the obstacle rather than the training itself.

Simple workout app FAQ

How many taps to start a workout?

One. The app opens on the Roll button. Tap it and you have an exercise and a rep count.

Is there an onboarding process?

One screen on first launch, explaining the dice, the streak and duels in three lines. Its button says "Roll the dice", and you never see it again.

Do I have to answer questions about my goals?

No. There is no questionnaire, no fitness assessment and no goal selection. The two dice settings are the only configuration, and the defaults work fine.

Will it ask for notification permission when I open it?

No. Permission is only requested if you switch the daily reminder on yourself. A fresh install prompts you for nothing.

Why does it only have twenty exercises?

Because the dice need a bounded, ordered pool to be meaningful. A thousand-exercise library is a thousand decisions, which is the exact problem the app exists to remove.

What is missing compared to paid apps?

Exercise demonstrations, a progression system, coaching and integrations. If you need a structured programme with video, a subscription app will serve you better.
