Diamond Push-ups

A narrow-grip push-up that shifts emphasis to the triceps. Meaningfully harder than a standard push-up, and the usual next step once ordinary push-ups stop being challenging.

How to do it

Setup

Take a push-up position but bring your hands together beneath your chest so your thumbs and index fingers form a rough diamond. Body in one straight line, midsection braced, feet a comfortable width apart for balance.

The movement

Lower your chest toward your hands, keeping your elbows tucked close to your ribs rather than flaring. Touch or come close, then press back up to a full lockout. The narrow hand position means a slightly shorter range but considerably more tricep demand.

Common mistakes

  • Flaring the elbows out, which defeats the point and stresses the shoulders
  • Hips sagging, as with any push-up variation
  • Hands placed too far forward, up under the chin rather than the chest
  • Wrists complaining because the narrow position increases the angle demand

Too hard? Scale it down

Do them with hands on a bench or a wall. Alternatively narrow your standard push-up hand position gradually over weeks rather than jumping straight to a full diamond.

Too easy? Scale it up

Elevate the feet, slow the descent, or move on to archer push-ups, which are further up this pool and considerably harder again.

Diamond Push-ups FAQ

Are diamond push-ups bad for the wrists?

They demand more wrist extension than a standard push-up. Doing them on fists, on push-up handles, or narrowing the hands gradually all reduce the strain.

How much harder than normal push-ups?

Most people manage roughly half to two-thirds of their standard push-up numbers. If you do thirty push-ups, expect fifteen to twenty diamonds.

Do my thumbs have to touch?

No. A close hand position is the point; a literal diamond is just a convenient description. If it hurts your wrists, widen slightly and keep the elbows tucked.
