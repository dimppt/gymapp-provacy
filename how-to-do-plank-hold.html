How to do it

Setup

Lie face down, then prop yourself on your forearms with elbows directly beneath your shoulders and hands forward. Tuck your toes under and lift your hips so the body forms one straight line from heels to head.

The movement

Hold. Squeeze your glutes, brace your midsection as if bracing for impact, and press the forearms into the floor. Breathe normally throughout, which is the part most people abandon. The moment your hips drop or pike, the set is effectively over regardless of the clock.

Common mistakes

  • Hips sagging toward the floor, which loads the lower back instead of the core
  • Hips piked up into an inverted V, which makes it easy and pointless
  • Holding the breath, which raises perceived effort enormously
  • Chasing time with a broken position rather than stopping when the line goes

Too hard? Scale it down

Plank from your knees, or with your forearms elevated on a bench. Both keep the position honest while reducing the load. A shorter hold with a perfect line beats a long one with a sagging back.

Too easy? Scale it up

Lift one foot, reach one arm forward, or elevate your feet. Alea will also draw holds up to three minutes on a large rep die, which is challenging enough for most people without variation.

Plank Hold FAQ

How long should I hold a plank?

Alea draws between 20 seconds and 3 minutes. Sixty seconds with a genuinely straight line is a solid general standard. Beyond about two minutes you are mostly training endurance rather than core strength.

Why does my lower back hurt during planks?

Almost always sagging hips. Squeeze the glutes hard, tuck the pelvis slightly, and shorten the hold. If it persists with a good position, have it looked at.

Forearms or hands?

Alea means the forearm version. The straight-arm variation is a valid exercise but easier for most people and closer to a push-up hold.
